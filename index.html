Dynamics of Interacting Particles, Common Noise, and Networks

The conference

When many particles, agents, or neurons interact, what survives the limit is not any one of them but a density — a curve in the space of probability measures. Making that passage rigorous, and then describing everything it discards, is one of the most active programmes in modern probability and analysis: fluctuations around the mean-field limit, common noise that no amount of averaging removes, hydrodynamic equations emerging from microscopic interaction, propagation of chaos and the interesting cases where it fails.

Over the last decade the same limit arrived from an entirely different direction. A wide neural network is a mean-field system. Training it is a gradient flow on a space of measures. A transformer is an interacting particle system on the sphere. The questions the theory of learning now asks — when does the limiting description actually govern the finite system, what do fluctuations cost, what does shared randomness do to a population of learners — are questions this community has been answering, in other words, for forty years.

Fluctuations in the Continuum puts the two in one room for five days in Berlin, and does it on purpose. A single track, no parallel sessions: everyone hears everything. Three opening overview lectures, each pitched deliberately at the other communities. Every day mixes particle systems, hydrodynamics and learning theory rather than segregating them into safe blocks. Eleven keynotes, around thirty-eight talks, twenty-five fully funded places for PhD students and postdocs, and enough unscheduled time for the conversations that are the actual point.

The interesting theorems are going to come from the overlap.
Come and help find them.

Topics

  • Mean-field limits and propagation of chaos
  • Fluctuations, large deviations and common noise
  • Interacting particle systems and hydrodynamic limits
  • Mean-field methods in machine learning: wide networks, training dynamics, transformers
